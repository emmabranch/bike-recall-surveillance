E-bikes

The fastest-changing part of bicycle recalls, and the one regulators are now catching up with.

From rare to routine

The first e-bike recall in these records is from 2001. For most of the next two decades there were a handful a year at most, and many years had none. Since 2022 e-bike recalls have appeared every year, and 2025 had more than any year before it. In total, 45 recall events involve e-bikes, e-bike batteries and chargers, or parts recalled from e-bikes.

Part of this rise is simply that there are far more e-bikes on the road than there were 20 years ago. These records do not include sales, so they cannot say whether e-bikes are recalled more often per bike sold than conventional bikes.

What fails on e-bikes

E-bikes inherit every failure a conventional bike can have, and add one of their own. The electrical system (battery, charger, motor, controller, and wiring) accounts for 33% of e-bike recall events. The rest are the familiar parts: forks, frames, brakes, wheels, and handlebars, now carrying more weight at higher speeds.

Battery fires

24% of e-bike recall events involve a fire or burn hazard, against 2% of other bicycle recalls. A battery fire is a different kind of hazard from a broken fork: it can start while the bike is charging indoors and unattended, and it can harm people who were never riding.

There is also a pattern in who is recalling. Of the 7 e-bike fire recalls since 2022, 3 name a distributor or an online marketplace in the notice rather than a bicycle brand. That fits a wider concern about lower-cost e-bikes sold directly online, but the number is small, and recalls only capture firms that cooperate with a regulator.

Regulators are catching up

  1. February 2021, Canada. Transport Canada repealed the federal definition of a power-assisted bicycle in the Motor Vehicle Safety Regulations. Since then, e-bikes have sat between federal vehicle rules, consumer product law, and provincial road rules, which may affect how e-bike problems reach Health Canada. (Transport Canada)
  2. March 2024, United States. CPSC published an advance notice of proposed rulemaking on e-bikes, the first step towards mandatory e-bike safety requirements. (Federal Register)
  3. June 2026, United States. CPSC proposed a mandatory safety standard for lithium-ion batteries in e-bikes and other micromobility products, covering batteries, chargers, and conversion kits. (Federal Register)

Recall counts after a new rule are hard to read. A mandatory standard can raise recalls at first, as non-compliant products are identified, before it lowers them.

About these numbers. E-bike recalls are a small series, so a single recall moves any year noticeably. Recall notices rarely state a bike’s top speed or motor power, so this project cannot reliably separate e-bikes from faster electric motorbikes sold as e-bikes. Canada’s e-bike recall count is very low, which may reflect the regulatory gap described above rather than safer products.
